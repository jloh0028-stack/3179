Exploring Australian Bird Populations

This project visualises how bird populations have changed over time in Townsville and Geelong, Australia. By exploring different years, you can see how bird distribution shifts, reflecting environmental changes such as habitat loss, urban expansion, or climate variability.

These patterns provide valuable insights for conservation and understanding ecosystem health.

What you’re seeing

This interactive bubble map shows the distribution of bird populations in Townsville. Each circle represents a location where birds were observed, with larger and darker circles indicating higher counts.

Use the year slider below the map to see how bird populations shift over time. These changes may reflect factors like rainfall patterns, habitat change, or urban development.

Use the zoom slider below the map to zoom out or zoom in on locations on the map.

  • Circle size: Total birds at that location
  • Colour: Darker = more birds
  • Watch clusters grow, shrink, or move through the years

*Counts are based on survey submissions and may vary by year and location.

Townsville vs Geelong (1998–2005)

Both regions show a clear decline in overall bird counts across the period. Townsville peaks in 2000, then trends downward, while Geelong peaks in 2001 before also declining.

  • Townsville peak: 2000 (highest annual count on the chart)
  • Geelong peak: 2001 (highest annual count on the chart)
  • Overall trend: Downward in both locations from the early-2000s

These patterns may reflect environmental change—e.g., climate variability, habitat change, rainfall shifts, or urban development.

*Counts are based on survey submissions and may vary by year and location.
